# Source One Findings: CBN Financial Stability Reports

**Source:** Central Bank of Nigeria Financial Stability Reports
**Periods Covered:** June 2022 — December 2025
**Extracted by:** Torvano Research
**Date:** September 2026

---

## What We Were Looking For

Three specific questions directed this analysis:

1. Are Nigerian banks dangerously exposed to real estate lending?
2. Is the overall banking loan book deteriorating?
3. Is speculative capital migrating into less regulated channels?

---

## What The Data Shows

### Finding One — Direct Bank Exposure Is Low and Declining

Commercial real estate loans as a percentage of total gross loans
has declined consistently across eight consecutive data points:

- June 2022: 2.50%
- December 2022: 2.12%
- June 2023: 1.96%
- December 2023: 1.99%
- June 2024: 1.76%
- December 2024: 1.57%
- June 2025: 1.43%
- December 2025: 1.35%

Residential mortgage loans remain negligible throughout.
Peak of 0.36% in December 2025.

**Conclusion:** A 2008-style banking crisis transmitted through 
real estate lending is not Nigeria's risk. The direct exposure 
simply is not large enough for that transmission mechanism to operate.

---

### Finding Two — Overall Banking Sector Stress Is Accelerating

Non-performing loans as a percentage of total gross loans:

- June 2024: 4.12%
- December 2024: 4.87%
- June 2025: 5.76%
- December 2025: 8.12%

This is not gradual deterioration. This is acceleration.
The ratio nearly doubled in 18 months.

Nonperforming loans net of provisions to capital — the measure 
of whether banks have enough buffer to absorb their bad loans:

- June 2024: 5.39%
- December 2024: 4.91%
- June 2025: 13.90%
- December 2025: 17.72%

That jump from 4.91% to 17.72% in 12 months means bad loans 
are growing faster than the capital banks have available to absorb them.

**Conclusion:** The Nigerian banking system is under broad and 
accelerating stress. Real estate is not the primary driver. 
But a sector already under this level of stress has reduced 
capacity to absorb any additional shock — including a real estate 
price correction.

---

### Finding Three — Speculative Capital Is Migrating

Real estate funds in collective investment schemes:

- June 2024: ₦98.61 billion
- December 2024: ₦99.95 billion
- June 2025: ₦361.88 billion
- December 2025: ₦501.24 billion

38.51% growth in 2025 alone.

While direct bank lending to real estate declined the same period 
saw institutional investors dramatically increasing indirect real 
estate exposure through fund vehicles.

Risk is not disappearing. It is migrating to channels that are 
less regulated and less visible than direct bank lending.

**Conclusion:** The reassuring headline — bank exposure declining — 
is accurate as far as it goes. It does not go far enough. 
The risk is becoming less visible not less real.

---

### Finding Four — Developer Financial Stress Is Acute

Construction and real estate sector commercial papers:
- 13 issues raising ₦47.51 billion
- Interest rates: 18.00% — 27.00%
- Tenure: Short to long term

Developers are borrowing at extremely high short-term rates 
to fund construction. At 27% interest any slowdown in property 
sales creates immediate and severe cash flow pressure.

**Conclusion:** The construction pipeline is under financial 
strain. The developers most exposed to a slowdown in buyer 
demand are already paying crisis-level borrowing rates.

---

## Overall Conclusion From Source One

Nigeria is not heading for a 2008-style banking crisis 
triggered by real estate. The direct bank exposure is too 
low for that specific transmission mechanism to operate.

But Nigeria does have:

- A banking system experiencing rapidly accelerating loan 
  quality deterioration
- Speculative capital migrating from regulated bank lending 
  into less visible fund vehicles
- A construction sector under acute financial pressure from 
  high borrowing costs
- Reduced systemic capacity to absorb additional shocks

A significant real estate market correction is building. 
It will not look like 2008 America. It will look like 
distressed developers, fund investors taking losses on 
overvalued assets, and a correction in premium property 
prices that creates opportunities for cash buyers with 
genuine market intelligence.

---

## What This Means For The Research Question

*Is Nigeria heading toward a real estate market correction?*

Source one cannot answer this completely. It shows the 
banking sector is not the trigger. It shows the system 
is already under stress. It shows risk is migrating to 
less visible channels.

Sources two through six are needed to complete the picture:
- NBS data to assess price sustainability
- Knight Frank data to assess rental yields and vacancy
- BOI data to assess development finance stress
- IMF data for independent systemic risk assessment
